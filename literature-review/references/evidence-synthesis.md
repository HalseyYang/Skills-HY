# Evidence Synthesis

## Appraisal Prompts

For each included source, ask:

- Is the source primary, secondary, guidance, standard, or opinion?
- Is the method appropriate for the claim?
- Are sample size, dataset, population, and setting adequate?
- Are outcomes directly measured or inferred?
- Are confounders, bias, uncertainty, and limitations addressed?
- Is the source current or superseded?
- Does the finding generalize to the user's context?

## Synthesis Structures

Choose the structure that best fits the review:

- Thematic: group by recurring claims, mechanisms, or concepts.
- Chronological: show how evidence or regulation changed over time.
- Methodological: compare methods, datasets, or measurement approaches.
- Jurisdictional: compare FDA, EU MDR, NMPA, IMDRF, ISO, IEC, or local regimes.
- Evidence strength: organize from strongest to weakest evidence.
- Gap map: organize by what is known, uncertain, and missing.

## Claim Discipline

Use calibrated language:

- "Strong evidence" only for consistent high-quality evidence.
- "Suggests" for limited or indirect evidence.
- "Associated with" for observational results unless causality is established.
- "No eligible evidence found" when search did not find evidence.

## Final Checklist

Before delivering:

- The research question is explicit.
- Search scope and assumptions are visible.
- Sources are cited and traceable.
- Included evidence is separated from interpretation.
- Limitations and uncertainty are stated.
- No citation details are invented.
- The answer fits the requested format and depth.
