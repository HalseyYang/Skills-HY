[P493] Section  B.1.6  Accessories or compatible devices
[P494] The accessory components of the product include a cerebral oximetry sensor, a peripheral tissue oximetry sensor, a pulse oximetry sensor, and a  wireless rSO2 sensor . For naming consistency, the term “Tissue Oximetry Sensor” is used as a generic designation encompassing the cerebral oximetry sensor, peripheral tissue oximetry sensor, and pulse oximetry sensor, all of which are intended for monitoring regional tissue oxygen saturation. In the present submission, the cerebral oximetry sensor is being submitted as an individual device.

[P506] Tissue Oximetry Sensor (rSO₂)
[P507] Tissue Oximetry Sensor (rSO₂) is a disposable device designed to be attached to the patient's head using its integrated adhesive. It functions by generating and detecting optical signals in cerebral tissue, which are then converted into electrical data and transmitted via shielded cables to the monitoring system. This enables real-time tracking of cerebral oxygen saturation levels.
[P508] The sensor operates with two wavelengths of light, each having an output power of 5 mW: 735 nm and 810 nm. It is intended for single use only. Reuse may lead to inaccurate or unstable measurements, complete signal loss, and an increased risk of cross-infection.
[P510] Compatible with all models BRS-100 / BRS-100A / BRS-100B / BRS-100C
[P511] Tissue Oximetry Sensor  (StO₂)
[P512] Tissue Oximetry Sensor (StO₂) is designed to be placed on the patient’s body for real-time monitoring of oxygen saturation levels in corresponding tissue regions. It operates with a light output power of 2 mW at both 735 nm and 810 nm wavelengths.
[P513] Other technical characteristics and usage methods of the peripheral tissue oximetry sensor are consistent with those of the Tissue Oximetry Sensor (used for the measurement of regional cerebral oxygen saturation). 
[P515] Compatible with all models BRS-100 / BRS-100A / BRS-100B / BRS-100C
[P516] Tissue Oximetry Sensor (SpO₂)
[P517] Tissue Oximetry Sensor (SpO₂) is designed to be placed on the patient's hand or toe for real-time monitoring of pulse oxygen saturation. It operates with a light output power of 5 mW at 660 nm and 5 mW at 905 nm. The data update cycle is 1 second, meaning that the pulse oxygen value is calculated and displayed based on data collected within each 1‑second interval.
[P519] Compatible with all models BRS-100 / BRS-100A / BRS-100B / BRS-100C
[P520] Wireless rSO2 sensor
[P521] Wireless rSO2 sensor is designed to be worn on the patient's head and utilizes Bluetooth 4.0 technology to establish a wireless connection with the host monitor. This enables real-time monitoring of cerebral oxygen saturation levels through wireless data transmission.
[P523] Compatible with all models BRS-100 / BRS-100A / BRS-100B / BRS-100C
[P526] Section  B.2 Specification of the Intended  Purpose, Target   Groups, Indications, and  Contraindications
[P527] Section  B.2.1  Intended purpose
[P528] This device is intended for continuous, non-invasive monitoring of regional cerebral tissue oxygen saturation, peripheral tissue oxygen saturation, and pulse oxygen saturation, with wireless transmission of regional cerebral tissue oxygen saturation data.
[P529] Section  B.2.2  Indication
[P530] This device is indicated for adult patients requiring monitoring of regional cerebral tissue, peripheral tissue, or pulse oxygen saturation.
[P531] The device is indicated for patients at risk of cerebral or systemic hypoxia where continuous monitoring of oxygenation is clinically relevant. Typical clinical scenarios may include, but are not limited to:
[P532] • Patients undergoing surgical procedures associated with potential cerebral or systemic ischemia (e.g., cardiovascular surgery, neurosurgery).
[P533] • Patients in critical care or intensive care settings requiring continuous monitoring of oxygenation.
[P534] • Patients in emergency care situations where cerebral or systemic oxygenation status needs to be continuously evaluated.
[P535] Section  B.2.3  Patient population
[P536] Adult ( ≥18 years old ) patients for whom clinical assessment of regional cerebral, peripheral tissue, or pulse oxygen saturation is recommended.
[P537] Section  B.2.3  Site in the body
[P538] Cerebral oximetry :  the site on the forehead above the eyebrow and must below the hairline where the Sensors will be linearly aligned.
[P539] Tissue oximetry :  site that provides the ideal access to the desired skeletal muscle tissue (if muscle cannot be palpated, too much adipose or edema may be present).

[P882] This activity is scientifically justified because the Non-invasive Cerebral Oximetry System is already supported by substantial clinical evidence, but new studies and publications may provide additional safety or performance data or highlight emerging risks. Regular, structured literature screening ensures that the clinical evaluation remains current and comprehensive, supporting the ongoing benefit-risk assessment. By using multiple reputable databases, standardized screening and extraction procedures, and data verification measures, the process ensures reliability, minimizes bias, and generates robust evidence to support regulatory compliance and patient safety.
